### Post 1: The Problem
> 1/ Why do experienced engineering teams get stuck during complex outages?
> 
> It's rarely a lack of data. It's "explanation lock-in." 
> 
> Once a leading hypothesis is chosen, it is assumed to cover the full incident. Teams stop seeing the anomalies that don't fit.

### Post 2: The Cognitive Reality
> 2/ The hypothesis doesn't become false. Its claim to completeness becomes insufficient.
> 
> "The explanation is still standing. It simply cannot hold everything we now know."
> 
> We wanted to design a tool that forces teams to confront this exact boundary.

### Post 3: Enter OUTGROWN
> 3/ OUTGROWN is an epistemic instrument. 
> 
> Instead of using AI to pretend it found the "root cause", it maps the gap between what you assume and what actually happened.
> 
> Let's look at the core interaction: the boundary contraction.

### Post 4: The Boundary Contraction (Media: x-loop.mp4)
> 4/ Watch this hero demo moment:
> 
> As predictions are tested, the supported evidence remains seated. The outside observations remain motionless. 
> 
> The claimed boundary contracts from "EXPLAINS THE FULL INCIDENT" to "EXPLAINS PART". The boundary yields; the facts do not.

### Post 5: Epistemic Integrity
> 5/ Notice how the investigation doesn't magically solve itself. 
> 
> No new culprit is named. The replication lag stays in open unresolved space. The final state is: SUPPORTED + INSUFFICIENT.
> 
> It prompts the user to do the only honest action: Reopen the Investigation.
